# Curated Reading Journeys

This document outlines the theological, pedagogical, and philosophical rationale for the curated reading journeys included in Scroll the Gita. Each sequence provides an offline, self-contained pathway through the scripture without artificial gamification, streaks, or completion pressure.

---

## 1. Gita on Duty (`gita_on_duty`)

**Focus**: Svadharma, Nishkama Karma (desireless action), and integrity in personal responsibility.

| Verse | Text / Context | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2.47** | *karmaṇy-evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana* | The quintessential shloka of Karma Yoga. Establishes the boundary between one's effort (which is in one's control) and the fruits of work (which are not). |
| **2.48** | *yoga-sthaḥ kuru karmāṇi saṅgaṁ tyaktvā* | Defines yoga as equanimity (*samatvaṁ yoga ucyate*) in success and failure during the performance of duty. |
| **3.8** | *niyataṁ kuru karma tvaṁ karma jyāyo hy akarmaṇaḥ* | Action is obligatory over total cessation of work, as bodily sustenance itself requires action. |
| **3.19** | *tasmād asaktaḥ satataṁ kāryaṁ karma samācara* | By acting without personal craving, human beings attain the supreme state (*param āpnoti pūruṣaḥ*). |
| **3.35** | *śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt* | Performing one's own natural duty, even imperfectly, brings greater spiritual stability than imitating another's path. |
| **18.47** | *śreyān sva-dharmo viguṇaḥ para-dharmāt sv-anuṣṭhitāt* | Reasserts the principle of natural calling (*sahajam karma*) in the concluding synthesis of the Gita. |
| **18.48** | *saha-jaṁ karma kaunteya sa-doṣam api na tyajet* | Acknowledges that every undertaking has incidental imperfections, like fire enveloped by smoke; one must persevere with right intention. |

## 2. Gita on Fear and Anxiety (`gita_on_fear_and_anxiety`)

**Focus**: Overcoming existential dread, grief over mortality, mental turmoil, and paralysis in crisis.

| Verse | Text / Context | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2.11** | *aśocyān anvaśocas tvaṁ prajñā-vādāṁś ca bhāṣase* | Sri Krishna addresses Arjuna's despondency by redirecting grief away from the perishable to the eternal. |
| **2.14** | *mātrā-sparśās tu kaunteya śītoṣṇa-sukha-duḥkha-dāḥ* | Contact with physical sensations is transient (*anitya*); cultivating endurance (*titikṣasva*) softens acute distress. |
| **2.20** | *na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin* | Deep affirmation of the immortal essence (*Atman*): unborn, imperishable, and unaffected by the destruction of the physical vessel. |
| **2.56** | *duḥkheṣv anudvigna-manāḥ sukheṣu vigata-spṛhaḥ* | The portrait of the steady mind (*sthitadhīr*): untouched by agitation during sorrow, and free from fear (*vīta-bhaya*). |
| **4.10** | *vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhā man-mayā mām upāśritāḥ* | Purification through absorption in truth, dissolving the trinity of inner afflictions: attachment, fear, and anger. |
| **6.5** | *uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ nātmānam avasādayet* | Elevate the mind by the mind; the self is its own ally when disciplined, and its own adversary when neglected. |
| **12.15** | *yasmān nodvijate loko lokān nodvijate ca yaḥ* | Neither disturbing the world nor disturbed by the world; tranquility free from anxiety (*harṣāmarṣa-bhayodvegair muktaḥ*). |

## 3. Gita on Equanimity (`gita_on_equanimity`)

**Focus**: Samatvam—steady mental poise amid life's dualities (*dvandvas*).

| Verse | Text / Context | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2.38** | *sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā lābhālābhau jayājayau* | Equating pleasure and pain, gain and loss, victory and defeat before acting prevents moral and emotional exhaustion. |
| **2.48** | *samatvaṁ yoga ucyate* | Explicit formulation: balanced awareness across varying outcomes is the core definition of yoga. |
| **2.50** | *buddhi-yukto jahātīha ubhe sukṛta-duṣkṛte* | Equanimity frees one from reactive clinging to past virtue and remorse over bygone missteps; skill in action (*yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam*). |
| **2.56** | *vīta-rāga-bhaya-krodhaḥ sthita-dhīr munir ucyate* | The sage of steady wisdom is balanced because emotional storms have quieted. |
| **5.18** | *vidyā-vinaya-sampanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini* | The mature vision of equality (*sama-darśinaḥ*): seeing the same divine consciousness in all beings regardless of social or biological form. |
| **6.7** | *jitātmanaḥ praśāntasya paramātmā samāhitaḥ* | The higher self is serene across environmental and psychological extremes: heat/cold, honor/dishonor. |
| **12.13** | *adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṁ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca* | Compassion and freedom from hatred paired with internal balance in joy and sorrow (*sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī*). |
| **12.18** | *samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca tathā mānāpamānayoḥ* | Equal toward friend and foe, unshaken by praise and blame, content in silent stillness. |

## 4. Chapter 2 in 7 Days (`chapter_2_in_7_days`)

**Focus**: A structured week through Chapter 2 (*Sānkhya Yoga*), the master summary of the entire Bhagavad Gita.

| Day | Verse | Key Theme |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **2.7** | *Kārpaṇya-doṣopahata-svabhāvaḥ* — Recognizing one's limits, vulnerability, and asking for true guidance. |
| **2** | **2.11** | *Aśocyān anvaśocas tvam* — The turning point: moving from sentimental grief to genuine spiritual inquiry. |
| **3** | **2.20** | *Na jāyate mriyate vā* — The timeless nature of consciousness (*Atman*). |
| **4** | **2.38** | *Sukha-duḥkhe same kṛtvā* — Meeting life's circumstances with balanced poise. |
| **5** | **2.47** | *Karmaṇy evādhikāras te* — The timeless mandate of selfless duty without craving the fruit. |
| **6** | **2.56** | *Duḥkheṣv anudvigna-manāḥ* — The character of the steady sage (*Sthitaprajña*). |
| **7** | **2.71** | *Vihāya kāmān yaḥ sarvān* — Attaining peace (*śāntim adhigacchati*) free from egoism and possession. |

## 5. Essence in 18 Shlokas (`essence_in_18_shlokas`)

**Focus**: One landmark shloka chosen from each of the 18 chapters to illustrate the progression of the Gita.

| Chapter | Verse | Theme |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **1.1** | *Dharma-kṣetre kuru-kṣetre* — The opening setting: the inner battlefield of conscience and struggle. |
| **2** | **2.47** | *Karmaṇy-evādhikāras te* — Sankhya & Karma: doing duty without grasping at fruits. |
| **3** | **3.19** | *Tasmād asaktaḥ satatam* — Karma Yoga: detached service as a pathway to the Supreme. |
| **4** | **4.7** | *Yadā yadā hi dharmasya* — Jnana-Karma-Sannyasa: the renewal of righteousness in human affairs. |
| **5** | **5.18** | *Vidyā-vinaya-sampanne* — Karma-Sannyasa: equal vision across all forms of life. |
| **6** | **6.26** | *Yato yato niścalati* — Dhyana Yoga: gently bringing back the wandering mind. |
| **7** | **7.7** | *Mayi sarvam idaṁ protam* — Jnana-Vijnana: everything is strung on the Divine like pearls on a cord. |
| **8** | **8.5** | *Anta-kāle ca mām eva* — Akshara Brahma: centering the heart on the timeless at life's threshold. |
| **9** | **9.22** | *Ananyāś cintayanto mām* — Raja Vidya: total dedication brings providence and inner protection. |
| **10** | **10.20** | *Aham ātmā guḍākeśa* — Vibhuti Yoga: the Divine seated within the heart of every living being. |
| **11** | **11.18** | *Tvam akṣaraṁ paramaṁ veditavyam* | Vishvarupa: realizing the transcendent and universal form. |
| **12** | **12.13** | *Adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānām* — Bhakti Yoga: true devotion manifested through kindness and equanimity. |
| **13** | **13.27** | *Samaṁ sarveṣu bhūteṣu* — Kshetra-Kshetrajna: seeing the same immortal spirit in mortal bodies. |
| **14** | **14.20** | *Guṇān etān atītya trīn* — Guna-Traya: transcending the three qualities of nature. |
| **15** | **15.15** | *Sarvasya cāhaṁ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo* — Purushottama: the light of awareness behind memory and knowledge. |
| **16** | **16.1** | *Abhayaṁ sattva-saṁśuddhiḥ* — Daivasura: fearlessness and purity as foundations of virtue. |
| **17** | **17.3** | *Śraddhā-mayo 'yaṁ puruṣo* — Shraddha-Traya: our character is shaped by what we place faith in. |
| **18** | **18.66** | *Sarva-dharmān parityajya* — Moksha-Sannyasa: the ultimate refuge and release from all anxiety. |
